## 01. The Poetics of Non-Interference

When we construct digital rooms for reading, we are constructing shelters from communicative overload. In classical Japanese tea architecture (chashitsu), the doorway is purposely low, demanding a bowed physical posture that strips the occupant of worldly status.

> "Silence is not the absence of sound, but the presence of an acoustic room wherein thought becomes architectural."

Hypertext should not be an escalator pushing the reader along an algorithmic funnel; it should behave as stone tiles placed across wet moss, intentional and quiet.

MARGINAL NOTE // REID, 1982 § 14.3

Hypertext should not behave as an escalator pushing the reader along an algorithmic funnel. Instead, deliberate information architecture acts as stone tiles placed deliberately across wet moss: each step is considered, autonomous, and physically anchored.
